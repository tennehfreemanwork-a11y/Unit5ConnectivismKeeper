CH.2The Designer's New Mission
The Network Keeper rewires the classroom — from content packager to network architect.

Connectivism demands a new role for instructional designers: stop packaging information and start architecting the network learners will navigate.

01 · Curate, Don't Just Create

Select, vet, and organize high-quality external resources. The course becomes a launch pad — not a destination. Point learners outward.

02 · Teach Digital Wayfinding

Source credibility and digital navigation become core learning objectives — not supplementary skills. Learners must know how to find AND evaluate.

03 · Design for Connection

Discussion boards, peer review, collaborative projects, and social annotation are structural features — not optional add-ons. Networks need nodes.

04 · Keep Content Current

Build living courses with updated links, real-time data, and learner-contributed updates. Knowledge has an expiration date — design for freshness.

05 · Embrace AI Nodes

AI tools, databases, and adaptive platforms are legitimate nodes. Integrate them intentionally rather than positioning them as threats to authentic learning.

06 · Assess the Network

Replace recall tests with portfolios, research projects, and networked presentations. Measure a learner's ability to find, connect, and apply — not memorize.

CH.3Battle Report: K–12 Literacy & Dyslexia
⚡ POWER UP — When connectivism works in K–12

"Oh yeah, I get the connection now — it's just like the time I saw..." Schema activated. Network is working. ✅

⚠️ DANGER ZONE — When it breaks down

"I don't have a laptop at home so I'll just catch up with the latest online gossip." Digital equity gap + no schema = network failure. ❌

⚡ STRENGTHS
📱

Multimodal pathways for dyslexic learners. Podcasts, videos, audiobooks, and interactive tools give students legitimate routes to knowledge that print-heavy instruction denies them.

🌍

Authentic motivation through real networks. When students connect to real researchers and community experts, literacy becomes purposeful — not performative.

👩🏾‍🏫

Teacher professional growth. Educators in under-resourced schools can build PLNs connecting them to structured literacy specialists their districts may never fund.

📖

Aligns with Science of Reading. Language-rich networked environments naturally increase exposure to varied vocabulary and text structures — a core SoR principle.

⚠️ LIMITATIONS
🧠

Cognitive overload risk is real. Dyslexic learners already expend heavy executive function on decoding. Open networks with ads and unfiltered results compound that load dangerously.

📶

Digital access is inequitable. Connectivism assumes broadband and devices that many K–12 students — including communities in Houston and Liberia — simply do not have.

🗺️

Young learners lack prerequisite schema. Effective navigation requires enough domain knowledge to evaluate sources. Beginning readers cannot yet distinguish quality from misinformation.

🔬

Theory precedes evidence. Connectivism lacks the empirical research base of behaviorism or cognitivism — raising questions about its use as a primary framework for foundational skills.

THE VERDICT:
LAYER, DON'T REPLACE

Connectivism is most powerful in K–12 literacy when used as a layer — not a foundation. Structured literacy instruction must anchor the experience first. Once students have that base, connecting their developing skills to real networks, authentic audiences, and digital tools extends the reach of what structured literacy makes possible. The network amplifies what the foundation builds.

— TC Freeman, LDTC 600, Unit 5
